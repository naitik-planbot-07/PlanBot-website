**Quick Answer:** Naitik Baldaniya (born 28 August 2009, Gujarat, India) is the founder of PlanBot and PlannextTech. He wrote his first program at 12, got the idea for PlanBot at the gym in May 2024 at age 14, coded it for 22 months while studying for a Diploma in IT, and launched on Google Play on 1 March 2026. ## The Gym Moment (May 2024)

Every founder story claims a lightbulb moment. This one is embarrassingly ordinary.

Naitik was at the gym when it hit him - not a business insight, a personal one: **his time management was a mess.** School, self-learning code, everything else - he was constantly busy and constantly behind. He had already cycled through the standard tools. The pattern he kept finding: every to-do app stores what you tell it, but none of them decide for you. The plan was always his job.

The thought was simple: *this is exactly what AI should do.* You provide the goal - "I have 4 hours, I need to finish X, Y, Z" - and the AI provides the plan.

He was 14. He started coding that month.

## 22 Months of Building as a Teenager

PlanBot was his first real project, and it shows in the timeline: about a year and ten months from first line of code to Play Store. No mentor, no team, no CS degree - just a diploma student in Ahmedabad learning by shipping.

Version 1, in his own words, was "broken everywhere." That is not modesty; it is the honest review every first build deserves. What matters is what came after: a long series of releases, uncountable rewrites (he has lost track of the version number), and one spectacular lesson in 2025 - he handed an AI assistant control of the entire codebase, watched it fail, and had to rewrite the whole app by hand. He now codes with AI assistance instead of delegating to it, and the difference shows in the product.

He also nearly learned unit economics the expensive way. In August 2026, keeping the server awake 24/7 for roughly 10 active users cost him about **Rs 2,000** in one month - real money for a student with no revenue. That month reshaped how he thinks about infrastructure, free tiers, and what "growth at all costs" means when the costs are yours.

## The Numbers, As They Are

Most founder blogs inflate here. This one does the opposite:

- **331 downloads** (as of 6 September 2026)
- **4.7-star rating** from 18 reviews on Google Play
- **10-20 daily active users**
- **Users in 3 countries**: India, the United States, Germany
- **Revenue: zero** - the focus right now is growth, not monetization
- **Team: one.** No funding, no co-founder, no employees. Friends and a college faculty member who supported the journey, but every line of code is his

Why publish small numbers? Because they are real, and because they set the baseline for everything that follows. The claim "10,000+ schedules generated" would be marketing. "331 downloads and climbing" is a promise you can verify.

## What He Actually Believes

- **"I don't care if they copy."** If a big player clones the core idea, so be it - the moat is execution speed, not secrecy.
- **India runs Silicon Valley's best companies, so Indians can build world-class products from anywhere.** His reason for aiming global from a small town in Kutch.
- **Marketing is the current weakness** - he says it himself. Sixty-eight blog posts in six months came from a Google Trends keyword strategy that worked (search traffic grew four figures in percentage terms), but paid ads and TikTok experiments failed: too small a budget for a solo student. The plan now is consistency over spend.
- **Gamification without gaming.** Oddly, he barely plays games - the streaks, XP, and Streak Freeze mechanics (inspired by Duolingo) came from studying what makes people come back, not from personal gaming addiction.

## The Human Behind the App

He speaks four languages - Gujarati, Hindi, English, and Kutchi. His first machine was an Acer Swift 3; his daily driver is a Realme P1 5G. His phone's home screen: YouTube, Chrome, WhatsApp.

Some honest contradictions he is fine living with:

- He built an AI time-planning app, and **his own time management is still a work in progress.** His daily routine "keeps changing every day."
- He preaches consistent revision. Before his own exams, he studies **one day before.** (He knows. He is working on it.)
- His parents know and support PlanBot - but are not entirely sure what "PlannextTech" is. Many founders' families would relate.
- When the internet goes down, his productivity system of choice is: sleep.
- Classmates know he builds an app, but in a system where "everything is education," nobody treats him as competition. He is fine with that too.

His favorite feature in the app is the one that started it all - the **AI plan builder**. The feature he personally uses daily is the **focus timer**. If he had to write PlanBot's slogan himself, skipping the marketing team he does not have: **"Just plan it with AI."**

## What's Next

Short term: a serious, consistent marketing push (the build was phase one; distribution is phase two), while finishing diploma finals and preparing for **DDCET** - targeting around 187 for a top engineering college in Ahmedabad. Class of 2027.

The scoreboard he plays by: **10,000+ active users** is the one-year goal, and **50,000 downloads** is his personal definition of "PlanBot made it." The five-year ambition for PlannextTech is stated plainly - a 100-crore company - and he is aware of exactly how hard that is. Hard is the point.

He is 17. The timeline above has roughly six decades left to fill.
