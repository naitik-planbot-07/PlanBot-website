**Quick Answer:** The JEE 2027 plan runs in four exam-anchored phases: Foundation (Oct-Jan, close Class 11 gaps + current Class 12 topics, ending with the January Session 1 as a diagnostic), Integration (Feb-Mar, finish the syllabus + previous year questions), Test Season (Apr, full mocks twice weekly before the April Session 2), and Advanced Push (the final 6-8 weeks before Advanced in May-June). Started in October that is about 9 focused months; start earlier and you stretch Foundation - the structure never changes. Study 4-6 focused hours daily as a school student, keep one weekly test-analysis day from week one, and let an AI planner handle daily rebalancing.

## Who This Plan Is For

This calendar is built for two profiles:

- **Class 12 students (2026-27 batch)** writing JEE Main in January/April 2027 while managing boards
- **Droppers** starting a dedicated 12-month cycle from October 2026

If you are in Class 11 right now and targeting JEE 2028, use this as a preview of your final year, not your current schedule. (We already have a guide on [creating a JEE study plan with AI](/blog/how-to-create-jee-study-plan-with-ai) that covers the earlier foundation years.)

## The Four Phases at a Glance

| Phase | Window (2026-27) | Focus | Mock tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Foundation | Oct - Jan | Class 11 backlog + running Class 12 syllabus | Chapter tests weekly; Session 1 (late Jan) as a diagnostic if you attempt it |
| 2. Integration | Feb - Mar | Finish Class 12 syllabus, start PYQs chapter-wise | 1 full mock every 2 weeks |
| 3. Test Season | Apr - May (run-up to Session 2) | Revision cycles + mock frequency doubles | 2 full mocks weekly + same-day analysis |
| 4. Advanced Push | Final 6-8 weeks before Advanced | Olympiad-level problems, weak-area surgery, formula sheets | 3 mocks weekly, all Advanced-pattern |

Wait - the exam windows. JEE Main Session 1 has historically landed in **late January** and Session 2 in **April**, with JEE Advanced in **May-June**. The 2027 notification is not out as I write this, so treat those as expected windows and confirm with the official NTA announcement. The plan below anchors to them.

Two starting points, one structure: a dropper starting in October gets roughly 9 focused months, and a school student who began after their boards in April gets the full 12. Starting later than October? Compress Phase 1 to six weeks by narrowing backlog scope - never by deleting the weekly test day.

## Phase 1: Foundation (Oct - Jan)

The single biggest rank-killer at this stage is carrying Class 11 gaps into Class 12 topics, because JEE papers freely mix both years. So the first 16 weeks run a dual track.

**Weekly template (Mon-Sat):**

| Day | Morning block (2-2.5 h) | Evening block (2-2.5 h) | Night (45 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Maths - current Class 12 topic | Physics - Class 11 backlog chapter | Formula flash review |
| Tue | Chemistry - Physical (current) | Maths - DPP of Monday's topic | Flashcards |
| Wed | Physics - current topic | Chemistry - Organic (Class 11 backlog) | Formula flash review |
| Thu | Maths - current + mixed problems | Physics - DPP + notes | Flashcards |
| Fri | Chemistry - Organic/Inorganic (current) | Maths - Class 11 backlog chapter | Formula flash review |
| Sat | Weekly cumulative test (3 h, timed) | Full error analysis, re-solve every wrong question | Plan next week |
| Sun | Buffer: backlog clearance, sleep recovery, light revision | | |

Rules that make Phase 1 work:

- **Never skip Saturday's test.** Chapter-wise tests from week one build exam temperament months before your first mock.
- **Backlog is a scheduled subject**, not guilt. Two evening blocks per week are reserved for it until it is gone.
- **Coaching/class homework merges into the evening block** - do not double-plan. One calendar, one source of truth.

## Phase 2: Integration (Feb - Mar)

Syllabus completion by the end of Month 7, with previous year questions entering the bloodstream:

- **Every finished chapter immediately gets its PYQ set** (last 10 years, chapter-wise). Knowledge that has never faced a JEE question is not JEE-ready knowledge.
- **Fortnightly full mocks begin** - yes, on an incomplete syllabus. Early mocks expose pacing problems while there is still time to fix them.
- **Short notes per chapter**: one page of formulas, traps, and "my mistakes." These become your revision fuel in Phase 3.
- **Chemistry Inorganic + Biology-adjacent memorization loads shift to night blocks**, when active recall beats problem-solving.
- **Late January = Session 1.** Take it if you are registered - a full-dress diagnostic, not the final verdict. A large number of top-500 scorers improve their rank massively in Session 2 - do not treat January as pass/fail.

## Phase 3: Test Season (Apr - May)

This is where the plan turns from learning to scoring:

- **Two full-length mocks per week** (Session-1 pattern), taken at the actual exam time slot so your brain learns the rhythm.
- **Same-day analysis, always**: every wrong answer gets a category - concept gap, silly mistake, or time pressure - and the category decides the fix.
- **Revision cycles run on your short notes**, not textbooks. Three full cycles across these months.
- **Between sessions (Feb-Mar):** rebuild weak chapters flagged by mock data, keep one mock weekly, then ramp to two before Session 2 in April.

## Phase 4: Advanced Push (Final 6-8 Weeks)

JEE Advanced rewards depth over coverage:

- Switch two of three weekly mocks to **Advanced pattern** (Paper 1 + Paper 2 format, multi-concept problems).
- **Problem quality over quantity**: Olympiad-style sets, matrix-match, paragraph-based questions.
- No new topics in the last 3 weeks. Only your notes, your error log, and your mocks.
- Sleep schedule locked to exam-slot wake times, 10 days out.
