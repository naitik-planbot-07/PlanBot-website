## Why CUET Changed University Admissions in India

CUET (Common University Entrance Test) is the single entrance exam for admission to 250+ central, state, and private universities — including Delhi University, JNU, BHU, and BBAU. Over **14 lakh students** appeared for CUET UG, making it one of the largest entrance exams in India.

Before CUET, Delhi University admissions were cutoff-based — meaning a single bad Class 12 board exam mark could shut you out of your dream college. CUET leveled the playing field: now, **your university admission depends on a standardized test, not your board percentage**.

That's a huge opportunity. It also means you need a strategy specifically built for CUET — not a generic board-exam approach.

## Understanding the CUET UG Pattern

CUET is modular. You don't write the whole paper — you choose sections based on your target university and course.

| Section | What it Tests | Subjects/Languages | Questions to Attempt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **IA** | Language (13 languages) | English, Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, etc. | 40 out of 50 |
| **IB** | Additional Languages (20) | French, German, Japanese, etc. | 40 out of 50 |
| **II** | Domain Subjects (27) | Physics, Chemistry, History, Economics, etc. | 35-40 out of 45-50 |
| **III** | General Test | GK, Reasoning, Quant, Current Affairs | 50 out of 60 |

**Key rules:**

- You can attempt a maximum of **6 subjects** total across all sections
- Each section gets 45-60 minutes
- +5 marks correct, -1 mark wrong
- Questions are **NCERT Class 12 level** for domain subjects

## The 5-Month CUET 2026 Master Plan

### Phase 1: NCERT Mastery (Months 1-2)

**Objective:** Complete and revise your Class 12 NCERT textbooks for chosen domain subjects

**Daily Study Target:** 4-5 hours

**The 80/20 Rule for CUET:** 80% of CUET domain-subject questions come directly from NCERT. If you master NCERT, you'll score well. This is the most underused insight in CUET prep.

**Daily Split:**

- Domain Subject 1: 2 hours (NCERT reading + MCQ practice)
- Domain Subject 2: 1.5 hours (if taking 2 domain subjects)
- Language (English typically): 30 min reading + vocabulary
- General Test basics: 30-45 min

**Key Actions:**

1. Read each NCERT chapter **twice** — once for understanding, once to make notes
2. After every chapter, solve 30-50 MCQs on that topic
3. For English, read 2-3 articles daily to build comprehension speed
4. Start a Current Affairs habit (15-20 min daily news)

### Phase 2: MCQ Practice & Weak Areas (Months 3-4)

**Objective:** Convert NCERT knowledge into MCQ-solving speed and accuracy

**Daily Study Target:** 5-6 hours

**What Changes:**

- Shift from reading to **solving** (3:1 ratio of solving to reading)
- Start sectional mock tests
- Identify and target weak chapters
- Build General Test skills (Reasoning + Quant)

**Section-Wise Strategy:**

**Domain Subjects (Physics/Chemistry/Bio/Math or Humanities subjects):**

- Solve 50-80 MCQs daily across your subjects
- Maintain an "error notebook" — every wrong answer goes in with the correct concept
- Re-revise weak chapters every Sunday

**Language (English):**

- 3-4 reading comprehension passages daily
- Vocabulary: 20 new words daily with usage

**General Test:**

- Reasoning: 30 questions daily (series, coding-decoding, puzzles)
- Quant: 20 questions daily (Class 10 level — simple but needs speed)
- Current Affairs: Continue daily news + monthly magazine

### Phase 3: Mocks & Revision (Month 5)

**Objective:** Full-length mocks, speed building, final revision

**Daily Study Target:** 5-6 hours

**What Changes:**

- 4-5 full-length CUET mocks per week
- Deep analysis after each mock
- Daily revision of your error notebook
- No new topics — consolidate only

**The Mock-Day Routine:**

- Morning: Mock test (as per your actual exam timing)
- Afternoon: Analysis — categorize every error (concept gap / silly mistake / time pressure)
- Evening: Fix the biggest error category

## Subject Selection Strategy: The Hidden Lever

**This is the most important decision in CUET prep** — and most students get it wrong by copying what "everyone takes."

**The golden rules:**

1. **Check your target university's requirements.** Delhi University's B.Com requires specific subjects; JNU's BA programs have different needs. Map this *before* choosing.
2. **Play to your strengths.** If you scored 95% in History and 75% in Math, take History as a domain subject.
3. **Don't over-diversify.** Taking 5-6 subjects dilutes your focus. 2-3 well-prepared subjects beats 5 mediocre ones.
4. **English + General Test is a powerful combo.** Many universities accept this for humanities and management courses.

## Common CUET Mistakes That Cost Seats

- **Ignoring NCERT for "advanced" books** — 80% of questions come from NCERT. Advanced books waste your time.
- **Taking too many subjects** — dilutes focus and lowers each subject's score
- **Not checking university subject requirements** — taking subjects your target course doesn't accept
- **Skipping the General Test** — even humanities students benefit; it's a high-scoring section
- **No mock practice** — CUET rewards MCQ-solving speed, not just knowledge

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is CUET harder than boards?** Not harder, but *different*. Boards test descriptive answers; CUET tests fast MCQ-solving. You need MCQ practice even if your board scores are high.

**Can I prepare for CUET alongside boards?** Yes — that's the ideal approach since both are NCERT-based. About 70% of your board prep transfers to CUET directly.

**How many CUET mocks should I solve?** 25-35 full-length mocks in your cycle, with the bulk in the last 6 weeks. Analysis matters more than quantity.
